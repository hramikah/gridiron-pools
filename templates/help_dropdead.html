💀Drop Dead Pool — How to Play

A survivor pool. One NFL team a week, picked to win outright with no point spread. If your team loses or ties, you are out — and each of the 32 teams can only be used once all season, so the easy wins run dry fast. Last one standing takes it.

The short version
1 team a week, to win
Straight up, no spread. Just win the game.
Each team once only
Use the Chiefs in Week 1 and they are gone for the other 17.
Lose or tie, you're out
A $30 buy-back is available in Weeks 1–4 only.
$20 per entry
70% to first, 20% to second, 10% to third.

1. Joining and paying

Already have a Gridiron Pools account? Open the Drop Dead rules page and press Join the Drop Dead Pool. New to the site? A commissioner sends you an invitation e-mail with a sign-up link, and all three pools are offered to you right after you register.

{{ h.figure("dropdead", "join-button.png", "The Join the Drop Dead Pool button at the foot of the rules page", "The one-press route: the join button at the bottom of the Drop Dead rules page.") }}

Everything happens on the home page. Every pool is listed there, and the ones you have not joined carry a Join button with the price on it:

{{ h.figure("dropdead", "join-home.jpg", "The three pool cards on the home page, each with a Join button showing its price", "The home page. Press Join Drop Dead Pool — $20 and confirm; the card then shows "You're in" and a button to make your picks.") }}

The entry fee is $20 per line. Send a check to Cedric Hay, 12300 US Hwy 19, Hudson, FL 34667. The pot pays 70% to first, 20% to second and 10% to third.

Signups close at the Week 1 deadline — Saturday noon Eastern of the opening week. After that the join button disappears for the rest of the season, for everyone. All three pools work the same way, so if you are thinking about playing, get in before the season starts. (The $30 buy-back is a different thing entirely — that revives an entry you already have, and it stays available through Week 4.)

2. Making your pick

Sign in, press Make a Pick and find the Drop Dead section. Every game that week is listed with both teams as buttons — you are choosing the one you believe will win, and the scoreline does not matter, only the result:

{{ h.figure("dropdead", "pick-grid.jpg", "The Drop Dead pick grid, with some teams greyed out and marked already used", "Both teams in every game. Teams you have already spent are greyed out and marked ALREADY USED.") }}

Press a team, then press Save Pick. Your saved pick then shows on its own with the matchup underneath:

{{ h.figure("dropdead", "locked-pick.png", "A saved Drop Dead pick showing the team and a remove button", "A saved pick. Press × Remove pick to take it back while the game is still open.") }}

Change your mind any time before the deadline or that team's kickoff window: press × Remove pick, confirm, and choose again. Removing a pick also gives the team back — it is only spent once the week is locked.

Keeping track of what you have spent

The top of your card lists every team you have used and how each one turned out, so you always know what is left:

{{ h.figure("dropdead", "teams-used.png", "A Drop Dead entry card listing the teams used so far with tick and cross marks", "Teams used so far. A tick is a team that won for you; a cross is the one that ended the run.") }}
The one-use rule is the whole game. Spending a strong favourite in Week 1 feels safe and costs you that team for the rest of the season — most people keep a couple of reliable teams in reserve for the weeks when the slate looks thin.

3. Deadlines

The weekly deadline

Saturday at noon Eastern. The countdown clock at the top of the pick page is the official one.

Each team's own lock

A team closes 1 hour before its game kicks off, so a Thursday night team must be taken by Thursday evening.

Forgetting to pick eliminates you — permanently. No pick saved by Saturday noon and your entry is done, and this is the one elimination a buy-back cannot undo. The rules are explicit: an entrant eliminated for failure to submit a pick may not revive the entry. Set yourself a reminder.

4. Elimination and buy-backs

Your team loses or ties, and your entry is marked eliminated with the week it happened. If that week was one of the first four, you are offered a buy-back:

{{ h.figure("dropdead", "eliminated-buyback.png", "An eliminated Drop Dead entry showing the Buy Back In button for thirty dollars", "An entry eliminated in Week 2, with the $30 buy-back offered before the next deadline.") }}

Press Buy Back In ($30) before that week's pick deadline and your entry is alive again. Points worth knowing:

  • It is available for eliminations in weeks 1 through 4 only. From Week 5 on, out is out.
  • You must buy back for each week you would otherwise have been eliminated. Two bad weeks means two fees.
  • The teams you have already used stay used. A buy-back revives the entry, not your team list.
  • It does not apply to an entry eliminated for failing to submit a pick.
  • No money changes hands on the site — the $30 is owed to the commissioners, the same as your entry fee.

5. How the pool ends

The competition stops when one player is left standing. There is one wrinkle worth understanding before it happens to you:

  • If five or more players are still alive and every one of them loses in the same week, the pool carries on — everybody survives.
  • If fewer than five are left and they all lose in the same week, that ends it, and the remaining positions are paid out on a pro-rata basis.
  • If a tie is still unbroken after all 18 regular-season weeks, the survivors keep going through the playoffs, choosing from the 12 playoff teams — again, each usable only once. If it somehow survives the Super Bowl, the pot is split equally.
