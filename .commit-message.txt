Standings: a tie ranks above no record at all

Hunter reported 0-0-1 entries scattered among the 0-0-0 ones on Master
Standings -- some above, some below, no pattern.

Gridiron scores a push as neither a win nor a loss, and the three Gridiron
sorts keyed on wins and losses only:

    rows.sort(key=lambda r: (-r["wins"], r["losses"]))

So 0-0-1 and 0-0-0 produced the identical key (0, 0). Python's sort is
stable, which means neither one was ordered against the other at all -- they
came out in whatever order the Entry query returned, roughly entry id, with
the pushed rows sitting wherever their id happened to fall. The same two
columns were also the whole rank key, so both records printed the same place
number.

Ties are now the last tiebreak, in the sort order and in the rank key:
most ties wins a level W-L. 0-0-1 above 0-0-0, and 5-3-1 above 5-3-0.
Applied in all three places that order Gridiron entries, so the season
table, the All Weeks grid and the per-week history agree:

  standings_gridiron          sort + _assign_ranks key
  gridiron_matrix             sort + inline rank key
  gridiron_record_through_week    sort

Wins and losses still come first -- a tie never outranks a win, and 0-0-3
still finishes behind 1-0-0 and ahead of 0-1-0.

Left alone deliberately: gridiron_awards(). Its Last Place tiebreak is
(wins, -losses, -ties) with best="min", which is Hunter's confirmed reading
of the printed rule and orders ties the other way on purpose.

tests/test_standings_tie_order.py covers all three call sites plus the
ordering-vs-record precedence; four of its five cases fail on the old key.
61 tests pass.
